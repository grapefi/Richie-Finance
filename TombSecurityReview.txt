TOMB CONTRACT REVIEW — 2026-10-03
================================

Verdict: NOT READY FOR SECURITY SIGN-OFF.

Scope: every Solidity source under contracts/tomb, their interfaces, existing
deployment/configuration wiring, and local execution tests. This is a source
review plus targeted integration/adversarial testing, not a formal audit,
formal verification, or explorer verification of live deployed bytecode.
No production contract was changed during this review. Oracle.sol stays 0.6.12.
Only review fixtures and tests, plus this report, were added.

Severity distinguishes ordinary-user exposure, admin-only powers, configuration
preconditions, and intended-but-risky design behavior. No claim of zero remaining
vulnerabilities is possible. Passing REPRO tests below means a bug exists.

COMPILER AND ARITHMETIC
-----------------------
Verified build artifact compiler IDs:
Oracle.sol (including its flattened libraries/Epoch): Solidity 0.6.12.
Peg, Share, GenesisRewardPool, ShareRewardPool, Boardroom, Treasury, PegRedeem,
RebateTreasury, PegPdaiZap: Solidity 0.8.20.
Genesis pragma is ^0.8.20; interfaces/IOracle pragma is ^0.8.10 and compiles
with 0.8.20. Other Tomb 0.8 contracts are pinned to 0.8.20.

Checked 0.8 additions/subtractions restore SafeMath-style revert behavior for
overflow/underflow. They do NOT ensure correct denominators, reward liabilities,
price windows, decimals, or economic incentives. Intermediate products in several
reward/getter/legacy LP formulas can still overflow and revert at extreme values;
full-precision Math.mulDiv is preferable where the final result fits uint256.

The two production 0.8 unchecked operations inspected are bounded: PEG subtracts
allowance after requiring allowance >= amount; Treasury decrements a tier counter
only while it is > 0. No wraparound exploit identified in these two operations.

Oracle's uint32 timestamp subtraction and uint256 cumulative-price wrapping are
intentional V2 arithmetic. Preserve those semantics when fixing Oracle in 0.6.
Consult's fixed-point multiplication explicitly checks overflow. Oracle is not
safe solely because these wraparound operations are intentional: window gating
is a separate and confirmed issue.

FINDINGS, IN PRIORITY ORDER
--------------------------

R1 — HIGH: Oracle does not enforce a minimum observation window.
Oracle.sol:582,675. Epoch scheduling gates clock boundaries, not elapsed time
since blockTimestampLast. A permissionless update at start+3599 followed by
another at start+3600 succeeds with a 3600-second period. The second stored price
is based on ONE SECOND. The operator can additionally make pre-epoch updates.
Local reproduction exercises the real 0.6.12 Oracle with cumulative-price
fixtures and verifies a stored price changes from approximately 20 to 1.
A combined real-Oracle/rebate reproduction nearly doubles the reward for the
same USDC deposit after replacing the full observation with a short low SHARE
price. Farm fee checks and rebate staleness checks accept the new timestamp;
timestamp freshness does not protect the observation length. Attack feasibility
and cost also depend on actual liquidity/reserve manipulation, not measured here.
Fix: enforce elapsed observation time >= period in Oracle.update(), maintain
valid counterfactual baselines, and constrain construction periods. Keep 0.6.12.
Also use market/liquidity limits; even a proper TWAP is not manipulation-proof.

R2 — HIGH, delayed-pool configuration: Genesis and Farm can over-accrue emissions.
GenesisRewardPool.sol:200 and Farm.sol:213. Active allocations are changed lazily
by updatePool. Updating an already active pool first pays its whole interval using
the old denominator; subsequently activating a delayed pool pays for an interval
overlapping that already-paid interval. Both real pool contracts are reproduced
with total accrued liabilities exceeding getGeneratedReward for the same time.
pendingShare can also overquote a not-yet-activated delayed pool.
Fix: disallow delayed starts unless implemented with global accounting that
splits intervals at activation times and uses a consistent historical denominator.
Current scripts reject delayed starts; the on-chain methods do not.

R3 — HIGH exposure/design decision: Treasury epoch catch-up compresses minting
and Boardroom lockups into consecutive blocks.
Treasury.sol:169,452. A successful allocation increments epoch by only one even
after many missed periods. With ten missed periods, five consecutive transactions
mint five compounded bootstrap allocations (>12% supply growth). Boardroom
lockups use this counter rather than a wall-clock holding duration. A new staker
can be eligible for backlog allocations and compressed epoch-based release.
This may be inherited catch-up behavior, but is unsafe if a six-hour interval
between mints/lockup epochs is intended. Decide that policy explicitly.
Fix: skip expired epochs, rate-limit actual mints, or settle historical epochs
without giving newly arrived stakes historical reward eligibility.

R4 — HIGH: Treasury accepts stale cached prices after a failed update.
Treasury.sol:382,452. _updatePegPrice catches all update errors; getPegPrice has no
freshness or nonzero validation. Outside bootstrap, an old above-peg price still
triggers expansion after many days and a failing update (confirmed locally).
Zero reserves, update errors, or keepers stopping can leave stale stored prices.
Fix: validate the PEG oracle pair/quote, observation age, nonzero price, and valid
window before using it for price-dependent minting. Failing closed must still
allow a designed recovery/epoch policy so Boardroom funds do not remain locked.

R5 — HIGH if underfunded: Genesis silently deletes unpaid rewards.
GenesisRewardPool.sol:304 and deposit/withdraw rewardDebt updates. safePegTransfer
pays min(requested,balance), including zero, then rewardDebt advances as if the
entire claim were paid. Funding the contract later does not restore the lost
claim. RewardPaid also reports the requested rather than actual amount.
Fix: preserve unpaid liabilities or revert atomically on insufficient funding,
without unintentionally preventing users from recovering stake principal.
Correct scripts fund first, but R2 can exhaust even an initially funded reserve.

R6 — HIGH, privileged/solvency risk: recovery functions can remove obligations.
Farm.sol:518 does not exclude SHARE; its operator can withdraw every funded SHARE
immediately. Rebates.sol:432 lets the owner withdraw SHARE already included in
totalVested; users' later claims then revert. Both are locally reproduced.
GenesisRewardPool.sol:315 allows removal of staked tokens after end+90 days even
if users still have recorded principal. These are ADMIN POWERS, not proof that
an ordinary user can call them. They defeat trustless reserve protection.
Fix: never recover tracked staking principal or committed reward liabilities;
restrict SHARE recovery to unencumbered surplus with an explicit emission policy.
Native PLS reserve withdrawal in Rebates is different: SHARE, not PLS, backs the
vesting obligation, so withdrawing PLS does not itself reduce funded SHARE.

R7 — HIGH if LP assets enabled: legacy rebate LP valuation uses live reserves.
Rebates.sol:392. A stale/TWAP underlying price is multiplied by immediately
changeable pair reserves. A constant-product reserve imbalance changes the quoted
LP reward by >5x without changing LP supply or the underlying TWAP in the local
arithmetic reproduction. Temporary reserve distortion can be quoted during
bonding, then reversed; this test does not execute a real flash loan/DEX swap.
Current configuration scripts reject enabled LP assets, but setAsset permits
them on-chain. USDC/PLS-only operation avoids this path.
Fix: reject LPs on-chain for this product or use manipulation-resistant fair-LP
valuation with separately validated underlying prices and economic bounds.

R8 — HIGH conditional token support: transfer taxes/rebases can impair principal.
Farm withdrawals subtract the requested stake amount but do not verify actual
tokens spent. A sender-side tax leaves recorded stake 50 units but only 45 units
in custody; the next full withdrawal fails (confirmed). Genesis fee transfers
have the same problem: received 100, fee 10, extra sender tax 1 => custody 89,
but credited stake 90 (confirmed). Negative rebases similarly shrink custody.
Normal recipient-side input fees are credited using received balance deltas;
that does not make ALL taxed/rebasing tokens supported.
Fix: restrict token behavior or verify custody deltas on every outgoing transfer;
unsupported behaviors must revert without creating undercollateralized claims.

R9 — HIGH conditional configuration: Genesis accepts PEG as the stake token.
GenesisRewardPool.sol:83 does not reject reward-token staking. An unfunded PEG
pool can pay rewards from the user's PEG principal, leaving a recorded stake
with zero custody; withdrawal fails (confirmed). Unlike Farm, Genesis also permits
zero token/reward addresses, leading to unusable deployments/pools.
Fix: reject PEG/zero/non-contract stake tokens and a zero reward token on-chain.
Current deployment/configuration scripts prevent the usual misconfiguration.

R10 — MEDIUM: add(false) applies new allocations retroactively.
Both add methods alter the denominator without first checkpointing old pools.
Accrued earnings can be cut by adding a new high-allocation pool before the old
pool is updated. Reproduced in both contracts. Always checkpoint regardless of
the flag, or remove/ignore the flag. Scripts currently pass true.

R11 — MEDIUM: emergency exits change past reward attribution.
Both emergencyWithdraw methods reduce totalStaked without updating reward
accounting first. Remaining users receive the prior interval calculated with
the reduced supply rather than their historical share (reproduced in both).
This forfeiture redistribution may be intentional, but is not time-weighted
accounting. Checkpoint first and specify how forfeited rewards are handled.

R12 — MEDIUM: Genesis pending view divides by zero when all allocations are zero.
GenesisRewardPool.sol:175. With nonzero stake and elapsed time, pendingRewards
divides by totalAllocPoint even when it is zero. Reproduced Panic(0x12).
updatePool/withdraw skip emission when zero, so this is not itself a permanent
principal lock. It can break UI balance reads. Guard the view like Farm does.

R13 — MEDIUM: Treasury's manual maximum expansion setter is overwritten.
Treasury.sol:311,436. Setting 10 bps then allocating outside bootstrap changes
maxSupplyExpansionPercent to 450 bps for the first supply tier (confirmed).
Expose a separate cap applied to tiers, or clearly remove the misleading setter.

R14 — MEDIUM configuration: Treasury assumes an 18-decimal PEG quote.
Treasury.sol:252 uses pegPriceOne=1e18 and 1e18 expansion scaling. PEG/PDAI with
an 18-decimal quote matches this convention. PEG/USDC does NOT: 1 USDC arrives
as 1e6 raw units, making the normal expansion comparison wrong by 1e12.
Do not reuse SHARE/USDC or WPLS/USDC rebate oracles as Treasury's PEG oracle.
Validate or normalize the intended peg quote decimals on-chain. Treasury's two
excludedFromTotalSupply addresses are hardcoded legacy values with no setter;
confirm/update them for this deployment rather than assuming they exclude Genesis.

R15 — MEDIUM configuration/liveness: Zap does not authenticate the LP factory.
Zap.sol constructor validates token0/token1, and the router's factory/WPLS, but
does not verify that the supplied LP is factory.getPair(PEG,PDAI). A fabricated
or wrong-DEX LP with those token getters can pass construction while the fixed
router uses a different real pair. Zap-out may fail. Check the registry address.
Constructor is covered by existing mocks; real PulseX swaps/LP mint/burn have
NOT been exercised by this review's local tests. Slippage/deadline inputs must
be chosen appropriately; zero minimums are not MEV protection. Selling half is
a convenience heuristic, not a fee/price-impact-optimal split.

LOW / OPERATIONAL NOTES
----------------------
* Share's stated 50,000 maximum omits initial deployer mint of 1 SHARE. Maximum
  schedule supply without burns is 50,000.99999999999424 (confirmed). There is no
  separate on-chain 50,000 cap. Resolve whether initial SHARE is extra or should
  be deducted from an allocation. No arbitrary public SHARE mint was identified.
* Genesis emits 2,399.328 PEG over 86,400 seconds, not exactly 2,400. Funding leaves
  0.672 PEG before empty pools, skipped allocations, and rounding. Farm emits
  40,999.999999999982976 SHARE over 370 days. TOTAL_REWARDS constants are not caps
  on liabilities and do not repair delayed activation.
* Each Share fund rate truncates 4,500/300days and leaves 2,880,000 wei per fund.
  The fund-claim arithmetic clamps end time correctly; rounding is downward.
* Oracle.twap divides by zero at the stored observation timestamp (confirmed),
  and returns zero rather than rejecting an unknown token. Epoch constructor
  accepts period=0 (which can make update loop indefinitely until out-of-gas).
  Guard construction and zero elapsed time while retaining 0.6.
* Rebate setShare and setShareOracle validate against each other's current value,
  preventing ordinary replacement of SHARE and its oracle in either order.
  Either make SHARE immutable or offer one validated atomic migration, only when
  totalVested=0. Discount, multiplier and vesting settings also need reasonable
  economic/overflow bounds; current owner inputs can make operations unusable.
* A zero rebating asset quote returns zero rewards (bond then rejects); invalid
  or stale prices reject deposits. Oracle quote units cancel between PLS/WPLS and
  SHARE; the USDC path correctly converts 6-decimal input to 18-decimal SHARE.
  These arithmetic checks do not resolve R1 or protect a poorly liquid market.
* PegRedeem exchanges equal RAW token units; it does not burn PEG or guarantee a
  dollar peg. The owner receives PEG and can withdraw PDAI reserves. Equal
  decimals/distinct valid tokens are required by scripts, but not constructor.
  Balance checks reject common input/output transfer taxes; anomalous extra
  sender-side PDAI taxes are not fully checked. Use the intended standard tokens.
* Boardroom exact-transfer checks intentionally reject taxed/rebasing core tokens.
  Snapshot bookkeeping, reward settlement, and Treasury permission wiring passed
  targeted integration. Epoch lockups depend on Treasury progress; failed keeper
  allocations/empty Boardroom bootstrap can stall progression. Claiming rewards
  resets the withdrawal timer, including partial withdrawals that claim rewards;
  this is the existing design, not a SafeMath regression.
* PEG's finite/unlimited allowance is always reduced in its custom transferFrom,
  unlike OpenZeppelin's special MaxUint256 infinite-approval behavior. Subtraction
  is guarded, and insufficient allowance atomically rolls back the transfer.
* Token owners can replace operators; Treasury/Oracle/pool governance can change
  critical settings. Transfer-administration does not move Share's dev/community
  fund roles. Use explicitly selected governance wallets; a new token owner is
  not automatically the new vesting-fund controller.
* Excess PSM payments are refunded under a reentrancy guard, and farm underfunded
  claims revert without deleting pending balances. Stake donations do not dilute
  recorded supply. Too many pools can make mass updates/harvestAll exceed block
  gas limits. Minimum claim thresholds can leave small final rewards unclaimable
  until governance lowers the threshold.
* The bondless core has no below-peg bond contraction mechanism. PegRedeem/rebate
  reserve ownership and oracle pricing do not independently guarantee a peg.

REQUIRED WIRING / ACCEPTANCE CONDITIONS
-------------------------------------
PEG mint operator = Treasury; SHARE operator = Treasury; Boardroom operator =
Treasury. Treasury administrative operator is the intended admin, NOT Treasury
itself. Boardroom and Treasury must reference the same PEG/SHARE and each other.
Genesis funded once from Peg.distributeReward(genesis); Farm funded once from
Share.distributeReward(farm). Rebate reserve must be funded separately and not
double-count Farm's 41,000 SHARE. PegRedeem needs separate PDAI reserves.

Pricing roles:
Treasury: PEG / intended 18-decimal peg quote (currently PDAI in the zap/redeemer).
Farm PSM: SHARE / WPLS (both 18 decimals); native PLS fee uses 10,000 bps scale.
Rebates: SHARE / bridged USDC; PLS option additionally WPLS / same bridged USDC.
USDC address: 0x15D38573d2feeb82e7ad5187aB8c1D52810B1f07, 6 decimals.
PDAI address in Zap: 0x6B175474E89094C44Da98b954EedeAC495271d0F (forked DAI).
Do not equate forked PDAI and bridged USDC or assume either always trades at $1.

Before deploying: resolve R1-R9 applicable to your supported configuration;
decide epoch/lockup/forfeiture policies; implement on-chain pool safeguards rather
than relying exclusively on scripts; retest all fixes and obtain independent
security review. Keep Oracle at 0.6.12 when correcting its window logic.
